Methodology

LabDessem Rust implements a hydrothermal scheduling model with thermal unit commitment, hydro operation with cascades, and optional network representation.

Model Scope

The current model supports:

  • Thermal unit commitment with startup and shutdown trajectories
  • Minimum up/down time for thermal units
  • Thermal residual TON cost treatment at horizon end
  • Hydro scheduling with cascades, water diversion, pumping plants
  • Two hydro generation formulations (FPHA cuts or productivity)
  • Dynamic maximum swallowing (EngMax) based on net head
  • Future cost function via Benders cuts
  • Generic renewable plants bounded by programmed generation
  • Submarket demand balance with optional DC network
  • Operational limit constraints

Objective Function

The objective minimizes total operational cost:

min Σ (thermal generation cost + startup cost + shutdown cost)
    + Σ (hydro spillage cost + turbining cost)
    + Σ (interchange penalty)
    + future cost (when FCF=1)

Thermal Unit Commitment

When UCT=1, each thermal unit has binary variables:

  • u[i,t] — commitment status (on/off)
  • v[i,t] — startup indicator
  • w[i,t] — shutdown indicator

Constraints enforce:

u[i,t] - u[i,t-1] = v[i,t] - w[i,t]  (state transition)
Σ u[i,τ] ≥ min_up_time × v[i,t]       (minimum up time)
Σ (1-u[i,τ]) ≥ min_down_time × w[i,t] (minimum down time)

Ramp Trajectories

Thermal units follow predefined ramp trajectories during startup and shutdown. The model reads trajectories from CAD_RAMPAS_TERMICAS.csv and enforces generation to follow the trajectory during state transitions.

Hydraulic Balance

For each hydro plant and period:

V[i,t] = V[i,t-1] + A[i,t] - Q[i,t] - S[i,t] + Σ(Q[j,t] + S[j,t]) - W[i,t]

Where:

  • V — reservoir volume
  • A — natural inflow
  • Q — turbining
  • S — spillage
  • W — water withdrawal
  • Summation over upstream plants in the cascade

FPHA Formulation

The Hydro Production Function Approximation (FPHA) linearizes the nonlinear relationship between generation, head, and turbining.

When FPHA="precomputed" or "computed", generation is bounded by piecewise linear cuts, one row per plant, period and plane (segment):

G[i,t] ≤ Fcorrec × (γ₀ + γV × (V[i,t] + V[i,t+1]) / 2 + γQ × Q[i,t] + γS × S[i,t])

Where:

  • G[i,t] — total generation of the units of plant i in period t (MW)
  • Fcorrec — correction factor of the segment: the alpha fitted by the computed mode, or the Fcorrec column of OPER_FPHA.csv. The row multiplies all four coefficients by it once.
  • γ₀, γV, γQ, γS — coefficients of the plane: intercept (MW), volume (MW per hm³), turbined flow and spillage (MW per m³/s). γS is not positive.
  • V[i,t], V[i,t+1] — reservoir volume (hm³) at the start and at the end of period t, the volume slots of the model (the balance above writes the end-of-period volume as V[i,t]). The plane reads the period-mean volume. In the first period the start volume is the initial volume.
  • Q[i,t], S[i,t] — turbined and spilled flow in m³/s. The model variables are in hm³ per period, so the row converts them with the period duration. Spillage enters as the lateral-flow term of the plane (Qlat.mw/m3s in OPER_FPHA.csv).

GeracaoMaxPontoMW in resultado_hidreletricas.csv evaluates the planes at the same period-mean volume; VolumeHM3 stays the end-of-period volume.

Cuts are read from OPER_FPHA.csv (precomputed) or fitted at run time (computed). A computed run writes its planes to resultado_fpha_computed.csv; the outputs section gives the rule that copies them into OPER_FPHA.csv, with Fcorrec = alpha.

Computed fit

For every hydro plant with generating units, the computed mode fits planes to the production function of the plant:

P = 0.00981 × η × Q × h_net          (MW, capped at the plant installed capacity)
h_net = forebay(V) − tailrace(Q + S) − loss

Where:

  • η — plant efficiency (Eficiencia in CAD_UHE.csv)
  • Q — turbined flow in m³/s, from 0 to Q_max, the sum of the maximum turbining of the plant units
  • forebay(V) — forebay level interpolated from the VHA table (CAD_GEOMETRIA_UHE.csv), not extrapolated beyond its ends
  • tailrace(Q + S) — one 4th-degree polynomial per plant in the total outflow (CAD_TAILRACE.csv)
  • loss — hydraulic loss in metres (Perda.m in CAD_UHE.csv)

The fit runs these stages in this order:

  1. Window — the fit covers the volumes [Vmin + min_volume_pct × span, Vmax − (1 − max_volume_pct) × span], with span = Vmax − Vmin of CAD_UHE.csv. The default is the whole interval. The window must lie inside the VHA table.
  2. Hull — samples the capped production function at zero spillage on a 5 × 5 grid of volumes over the window and flows over [0, Q_max], and keeps the upper facets of the three-dimensional convex hull as planes γ₀ + γV×V + γQ×Q.
  3. γS secant — for each plane, takes the grid point where that plane is the lowest of all planes and fits γS as the least-squares slope of the uncapped production function against spillage there, over 9 samples between 0 and 2 × MLT, the long-term mean inflow of the plant in m³/s.
  4. Reduction within the plane limit — merges planes whose normals differ by less than the angle tolerance (1.0 degree by default). While more planes than the plane limit (10 by default) remain, it doubles the tolerance and merges again from the original planes, at most 32 times. A limit it cannot reach fails the plant. The plane limit is a labdessem extension, not DESSEM behavior.
  5. Alpha — fits the correction factor alpha by least squares on 30 evaluation points of the final planes: 6 volumes × 5 flows, or 2 volumes × 15 flows for a plant with a single-volume table. alpha therefore depends on the plane limit.
  6. Deviation — measures the maximum, mean absolute and root-mean-square difference between alpha × the lowest plane and the production function on the fit grid at zero spillage, and writes them to resultado_fpha_computed.csv.
  7. Validation — fails the plant for an empty plane set, a non-positive alpha or a coefficient with the wrong sign (γV or γQ negative, γS positive).

A plant that cannot be fitted stops the run before any result is written. For the fitting options see CAD_FPHA_CONFIG.csv; for the failure messages and fixes see Computed FPHA failures. The detail of every stage, the decision records and the known limitations are in docs/design/fpha-formulation.md.

Constant productivity

When FPHA="constant_productivity", generation is computed directly:

G[i,t] = ρ × Q[i,t]

Where ρ is the specific productivity (MW per m³/s).

Dynamic Maximum Swallowing (EngMax)

When EngMax=1, the total turbining of each hydro plant with units is bounded in every period by a head-dependent maximum swallowing. One row is added per plant and period (MM eq. 9.34):

Σᵤ Q[u,t] ≤ EngMax[p,t] = Q_ef × (h_l / h_ef)^α

Where:

  • Q_ef — maximum turbining at effective head: the sum of the plant's units (from registration)
  • h_ef — effective head: the unit-count-weighted mean of the plant's units (from registration)
  • h_l — net head in metres, calculated from the previous solve
  • α — turbine exponent (0.5 for Francis, Pelton and unknown turbine types; 0.2 for Kaplan)

Net head is calculated as:

h_l = forebay_level(V) - tailrace_level(Q_jus) - hydraulic_loss

Where V is the average reservoir volume of the period and Q_jus is the plant outflow (turbining plus spillage, m³/s), both taken from the previous solve, and the hydraulic loss is in metres. The forebay level is interpolated from the VHA table (CAD_GEOMETRIA_UHE.csv), and the tailrace level is computed from a 4th-degree polynomial in Q_jus (CAD_TAILRACE.csv):

tailrace = a₀ + a₁×Q_jus + a₂×Q_jus² + a₃×Q_jus³ + a₄×Q_jus⁴

The plant row is added beside the static per-unit turbining limits, so the tighter of the two applies. The max swallowing bound is not capped at Q_ef: it exceeds Q_ef when the net head is above the effective head. When geometry data is unavailable, the effective head from registration is used.

Bounds are recalculated after every solve (LP, MILP, LP-FIXED and LP-CALC-CMO) from the solution of the solve immediately before it. The first solve uses the initial volume, and the plant maximum turbining with no spillage.

Future Cost Function

The Future Cost Function (FCF) represents the expected cost-to-go from the end of the horizon. It is approximated by Benders cuts:

α ≥ π₀ + Σ πᵢ × Vfinal[i]

Where:

  • α — future cost variable (added to objective)
  • π₀ — cut intercept
  • πᵢ — coefficient for final volume of hydro plant i

Cuts are read from binary file OPER_FCF.bin when FCF=1.

DC Network Flow

When rede=1, the model includes DC power flow approximation:

f[l,t] = (θ[from] - θ[to]) / x[l]  (line flow)
|f[l,t]| ≤ f_max[l]                 (thermal limit)

Bus power balance enforces generation minus demand equals net injection.

Iterative Network Cuts

In execution mode 1, network constraints are added iteratively:

  1. Solve LP relaxation (network-free)
  2. Compute DC flows from solution
  3. Identify violated line-period pairs
  4. Add flow constraints for violations
  5. Repeat until no violations or iteration limit
  6. Solve final MILP with all cuts
  7. Fix integers and solve LP-CALC-CMO for duals

This approach keeps the model size manageable while ensuring feasibility.
